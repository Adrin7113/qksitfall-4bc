Two drivers, different deadlines

A has a 50 kWh battery at 30%, targets 50%, leaves at 07:45, and must finish by 10:00. B starts at 12%, targets 30%, leaves at 07:50, and must finish by 09:00.

Both use 0.2 kWh/km. Arrival reserve must be at least 5% (2.5 kWh).

Travel and charging change the options

A → North: 2 km / 10 min; A → South: 5 km / 15 min.
B → North: 1 km / 5 min; B → South: 20 km / 30 min.

At North, A needs 10.4 kWh and B needs 9.2 kWh. At South, A needs 11 kWh. B reaches South below reserve, after the early slot starts, and cannot finish in time.

Tariffs, then feasibility

Both chargers supply 11 kW. North costs ₹2/kWh early and ₹1 late; South costs ₹3 early and ₹2 late.

Before scoring, reject bookings that break reserve, arrival, completion deadline, or one-hour charging capacity. Pay only for energy needed to reach the target.

TAKEAWAY

The cheapest individual booking can block another driver. A compatible station-and-time combination serves both cars.

03 / THE QUANTUM MODEL

Turn charging choices into an energy problem.

ACTUAL QISKIT CIRCUITS

QUBO defines the optimization problem. It scores selected plans and penalizes broken constraints. QAOA searches for low-energy candidates with a quantum circuit and a classical parameter optimizer.

Binary choices→QUBO costs→Ising Hamiltonian→QAOA + validation
WHAT QUANTUM CONTRIBUTES

Charging rules become interactions between qubits.

We encode charging-plan compatibility into a quantum search problem and evaluate its performance against classical alternatives.

01 / CLASSICAL

Generate feasible plans

Check each car’s energy, arrival, deadline and power ceiling. One bit represents a complete module, gun and power profile.

02 / ENCODING

Build QUBO costs

Charge for time until completion. Penalize missing cars, multiple plans and incompatible pairs.

03 / QUANTUM MODEL

Couple the plan qubits

Map x = (I − Z)/2. RZ and RZZ rotations apply the cost; RX mixing moves amplitude between selections.

04 / HYBRID LOOP

Tune, sample, validate

Classical COBYLA tunes four angles over two layers. Ideal Aer evaluates states locally. Accept only physically valid measured plans.

Our engineering contribution is the charging-specific encoding and checked hybrid workflow. We use standard QAOA methods. Superposition and interference change the sampling distribution; they do not guarantee an optimum or a speed advantage. The current X mixer can also produce invalid selections.

The energy equation

E(x) = Σ cᵢxᵢ + P[(x₀+x₁+x₂+x₃−1)² + (x₄−1)² + x₀x₄]

cᵢ is each booking's score. P = 57.65, calculated from the current booking scores by the Python model. The squared terms require one booking per car. x₀x₄ penalizes sharing North's early slot.

COST FUNCTION

Electricity spending plus a preference for less waiting.

For booking i: cᵢ = Eᵢ pᵢ + λ wᵢ. E is charging energy in kWh, p is electricity price per kWh, w is waiting in minutes, and λ is the waiting preference. The combined score is in preference points.

Minimize C(x), subject to:

x₀ + x₁ + x₂ + x₃ = 1   ·   x₄ = 1   ·   x₀x₄ = 0

One booking for A; one for B; no double booking at North in the early slot.

QUBO

Make invalid booking combinations expensive.

The squared terms enforce exactly one booking per car. The product x₀x₄ penalizes the shared North/early charger. The implementation chooses P = 1 + the sum of each car’s maximum booking score, providing a safe bound for this nonnegative-cost model.

ISING HAMILTONIAN

Translate binary decisions into qubit operators.

Substitute xᵢ → (I − Zᵢ)/2. I is the identity operator and Zᵢ is Pauli-Z on qubit i. Eigenvalue +1 represents xᵢ = 0; eigenvalue −1 represents xᵢ = 1.

Every computational basis state has the same energy as its QUBO value. A lowest-energy state therefore encodes an optimal schedule for this model.

Normalization used by the actual circuit View +

The constant identity term contributes only a global phase, so the circuit omits it. Dividing by a positive scale preserves the minimizing schedule.

THE SHARED-POWER QUBO / COSTS IN MINUTES
F(x) = ∑ cᵣxᵣ + P ∑ᵢ (∑ᵣ∈Rᵢ xᵣ − 1)² + P ∑₍ᵣ,ₛ₎∈K xᵣxₛ

cᵣ = completion − arrivalMinimize total time; with every car served, its average has the same minimizer.

Rᵢ = plans for car iThe squared terms penalize missing cars and selecting multiple plans for one car.

K = incompatible plan pairsReject overlapping use of one gun and combined module power above 50 kW.

P = 121 exceeds the maximum feasible schedule cost, 120. Valid selections have zero penalty. This pairwise encoding relies on exactly two guns per module and independent module power budgets.

MEASURED CONCLUSION

      
SAMPLING BEHAVIOR
A changed distribution is not a speedup.
Structured random already enforces one plan per car. Comparing QAOA only with unrestricted random bits would give a misleading picture of optimization value.
WHAT REMAINS UNTESTED
Hardware and scaling benefits are hypotheses.
These 6–12 qubit cases are small. We have not tested QPUs, noise, larger fleets or a quantum advantage. Equal final samples do not equal equal computational work: QAOA also pays for training.

      
Protocol, timings and reproducibility Inspect evidence +
Same physical inputs, appropriate formulations
All methods use identical generated plans. QAOA: 2 layers, 80 evaluations per restart, 2 restarts, 2,048 final shots, seeds 0–9. Structured random: 2,048 one-plan-per-car draws per seed. Native MILP: SciPy/HiGHS with assignment, gun occupancy and module power constraints, repeated 10 times.
Every returned schedule is checked independently. MILP optima are checked against the existing exact reference. Error and no-feasible-answer runs stay in the report.
Complete workflow runtime
Candidate generation, formulation, training/solving/sampling and validation are timed. Interpreter and import startup, and exact-reference computation, are excluded. Process CPU time sums threads; elapsed time measures wall time. Aer time is simulation runtime, never QPU runtime.
Aer uses one thread; HiGHS retains its defaults. Methods rotate order between seeds; no CPU affinity or resource isolation is enforced. This is a reproducible small-instance comparison, not a broad performance study.
The protocol follows principles from Towards Robust Benchmarking of Quantum Optimization Algorithms: native formulations, transparent costs and classical baselines. Broader instance sets and matched tuning budgets remain future work. Run python scripts/benchmark_quantum.py offline to regenerate the versioned JSON.

      
WHAT WE HAVE IMPLEMENTED
Two decision models and their quantum mappings.
Station-and-time booking, shared-power scheduling, QUBO-to-Ising conversion, actual QAOA circuits, and independent feasibility checks. Validation includes 63 Python tests and a bounded stress audit of 90,336 assignments.
Current conclusion: QAOA found the optimum in 49 of 50 runs; MILP and structured random each did so in all 50. These results demonstrate no quantum advantage.
